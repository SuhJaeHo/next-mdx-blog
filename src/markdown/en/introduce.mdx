## Stability and Ownership Learned from Real-Time Trading

At Wemade, I helped develop the web frontend of an order-book-based game-token exchange. In a small team, I participated from initial design through launch and operations. When key contributors left during the project, I rapidly learned the existing architecture and trading logic and continued operating the service with the remaining team.

I built a real-time price chart by combining historical market APIs with WebSocket streams. For high-frequency order-book updates, I reduced rendering load through buffering and batched updates, then separated update priorities using React's concurrent rendering capabilities. The interface remained responsive even under bot tests designed to simulate heavy traffic.

This experience taught me that frontend engineering is not merely screen development. It requires considering networks, data flow, and browser rendering together to create the stability users actually experience.

## Practical Architecture for the Situation at Hand

At NEXUS, I developed products with very different characteristics: a wallet, an exchange, a public-sale platform, and a staking service. I prioritize architecture appropriate to the schedule, team, and operating environment—not simply the newest or most elaborate solution.

For a public-sale service, I delivered project setup, wallet connection, KYC, RPC configuration, and smart-contract calls in one week. When public RPC rate limits delayed data retrieval during operations, I identified the cause and migrated to a JWT-authenticated private RPC. The service completed a public sale worth approximately KRW 20 billion without a critical incident.

For a token exchange, I unified duplicated transaction logic behind an adapter and migrated the application from a monorepo into an independent project. I also built generation of DTOs, entities, and repositories from Swagger specifications. These changes preserved all existing behavior while reducing the codebase by approximately 30%.

To me, practical engineering does not mean insisting on a particular technology or design pattern. It means avoiding unnecessary abstraction and excessive complexity while choosing a structure that is easy to change and maintain.

My strengths are quickly understanding unfamiliar systems, dividing problems into solvable units, and delivering a working product within a fixed schedule. I will continue to put the problem and the value delivered to users before the technology itself, building products that are stable and maintainable.
